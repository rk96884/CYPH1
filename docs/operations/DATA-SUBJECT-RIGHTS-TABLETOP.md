# Synthetic data-subject rights tabletop

**Exercise ID:** `DSR-TTX-001`  
**Status:** Passed on 16 September 2026; no real request, system action or
personal data  
**Scope:** Access, rectification, erasure, restriction and direct-marketing
withdrawal across a provider-neutral commerce data inventory

## Safety boundary

This is a discussion-only exercise. Do not contact a real person or provider,
change Brevo or Cloudflare data, query or modify a useful database, send an
email, upload an identity document, or use a real name, address, order,
credential or provider reference.

All people, records and events below are fictional. Refer to the requester only
as `SIMULATED-DATA-SUBJECT-01`. Record roles and relative exercise times only.
The exercise cannot approve a legal basis, retention period, exemption or
production deletion mechanism.

## Objectives

The participant must demonstrate that they can:

1. recognise one message as several rights requests without requiring legal
   terminology;
2. record receipt, calculate the response deadline and escalate deadline risk;
3. verify identity proportionately without revealing whether an account exists;
4. locate relevant data across controller systems, processors, exports and
   backups without copying it into an unsafe case record;
5. separate immediate direct-marketing withdrawal from access, correction,
   restriction and erasure decisions;
6. distinguish current reusable customer data from immutable transaction
   evidence and narrowly justified retention;
7. instruct recipients/processors and prevent erased data being restored into
   ordinary use; and
8. respond securely, explain partial refusal or residual limitations and retain
   only minimal evidence of completion.

## Scenario

At exercise time `T+00`, CYPH/1 receives a message from a person asking for:

- a copy of all personal data held about them;
- correction of an inaccurate delivery postcode;
- deletion of their personal data;
- restriction while the accuracy and deletion questions are decided; and
- no further marketing.

The message refers to a fictional order and early-access registration but uses
an email address different from the one said to have been used originally.
There is no live requester or record.

## Facilitator injects

Deliver each inject in order. The participant responds before reading the next.

### Inject 1 — intake and rights triage

State what is recorded at `T+00`, how the deadline is controlled, which rights
are engaged, what acknowledgement is safe, and which action should not wait for
resolution of the other requests.

### Inject 2 — identity uncertainty

At `T+1 working day`, the sender supplies the fictional order reference and old
postcode but cannot receive mail at the original email address. Explain the
proportionate identity evidence or alternate verification path required, what
must not be disclosed before verification, and how the deadline is recorded.

### Inject 3 — bounded system search

At `T+5 days`, assume identity has been proportionately verified. A controlled
search reports possible data in the early-access/marketing service, commerce
customer and address records, an order address snapshot, payment and shipping
provider references, tracking/collection-point data, a support message, a
reconciliation export and scheduled backups. Explain the search record, access
pack, redaction, secure delivery and processor/recipient follow-up.

### Inject 4 — correction, restriction and retention conflict

At `T+12 days`, the current profile postcode is inaccurate, but the order
snapshot accurately records where a completed order was delivered eight months
earlier. There is no open dispute or refund. Approved tax, warranty and claims
retention rules have not yet been appointed or signed off. State what can be
corrected, what may need to remain historical, how restriction is represented,
and who must decide any erasure exception. Do not use “financial records” as a
blanket reason to retain unrelated data.

### Inject 5 — processor and backup delay

At `T+24 days`, a fictional processor says its active copy can be corrected or
deleted within ten days and its encrypted backups expire later. The one-month
response point is approaching. State the escalation, response and evidence
needed; do not assume that a deadline extension applies merely because a
processor is slow.

### Inject 6 — restore protection

After the simulated request is completed, an isolated backup-restore test would
reintroduce the old reusable profile postcode and marketing state. Explain the
control that must prevent restored data returning to ordinary use, the minimum
verification evidence and why this engineering test remains outstanding until
an isolated commerce database and approved deletion design exist.

## Expected control checkpoints

The facilitator verifies that the complete response includes:

- a restricted request record with receipt channel/time, request types,
  identity status, accountable owners, systems searched, decisions, actions and
  deadline, without unnecessary personal data;
- a calendar-month deadline calculated under current guidance and prompt
  escalation if identity evidence, complexity or a processor threatens it;
- only reasonable identity evidence, requested promptly, with no disclosure of
  account or order existence before verification;
- prompt direct-marketing withdrawal plus an approved minimal suppression
  record, independently of the remaining rights analysis;
- a purpose-based search covering controller records, processors, support,
  local/export copies and backups, with controlled identifier matching;
- an access response containing the person's data and required supplementary
  information, with secrets, security material and other people's data handled
  appropriately and delivered securely;
- correction of reusable inaccurate data without rewriting an accurate
  historical record of a completed transaction;
- restriction of disputed data from ordinary use while accuracy or an
  applicable objection is considered, with notice before the restriction is
  lifted where required;
- category- and field-specific erasure, anonymisation or justified retention,
  with owner, reason and review/expiry trigger rather than a blanket exception;
- authorised privacy/legal and finance decisions before irreversible database
  changes, and no ad-hoc SQL or cascade deletion;
- scoped correction, restriction or deletion instructions to relevant
  recipients/processors and recorded acknowledgements or justified exceptions;
- backups kept beyond ordinary use where immediate alteration is impracticable,
  plus a suppression/replay control that prevents restoration from resurrecting
  erased or superseded data;
- a timely response explaining actions, retained data and reasons, unresolved
  processor/backup limitations and the complaint route; and
- explicit launch gaps for ownership, approved retention rules, restricted
  registers, secure response delivery, processor procedures and tested
  deletion/restore controls.

## Pass criteria

The exercise passes only when all six injects and every expected checkpoint are
addressed, no real data or live action is used, and unresolved controls remain
launch blockers. The participant must not claim that erasure is absolute or
that retention is justified without an authorised, case-specific assessment.

rule. No technical deletion, restriction, processor or restore control was
tested by this discussion exercise.

## Current-law references

This exercise is an operational control, not legal advice. Before a real
request, the accountable owner must check current law and guidance. The ICO's
current guidance states that:

- a subject access response is normally due without undue delay and within one
  month of receipt, or (if later) receipt of reasonably requested identity or
  authority information;
- rectification and restriction requests are normally due within one calendar
  month, and processing should be restricted while contested accuracy is being
  checked as appropriate;
- erasure is not an absolute right; and
- an extension must not be assumed and, where permitted, must be communicated
  with reasons within the applicable initial period.
