# CYPH/1 pre-launch traffic strategy

**Status:** Proposed  
**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Scope:** Drive relevant UK traffic to the CYPH/1 pre-launch website and convert interested visitors into confirmed early-access subscribers.

## 1. Objective

The immediate goal is not maximum traffic. It is to attract people with a genuine interest in premium at-home IPL, help them understand the CYPH/1 point of view and earn permission to continue the relationship by email.

Success should be judged primarily by:

- confirmed early-access subscribers;
- the percentage of relevant visitors who confirm their subscription;
- subscriber quality, reflected in low unsubscribe and complaint rates; and
- what each campaign teaches us about the audience, message and eventual product direction.

## 2. Strategic principles

1. **Education before promotion.** Lead with useful, evidence-led explanations of the hair-growth cycle, consistency and responsible use of home IPL.
2. **Build trust before reach.** A smaller audience that understands the proposition is more valuable than large volumes of low-intent traffic.
3. **Organic learning before paid scale.** Test messages and formats organically, then put limited paid support behind the strongest performers.
4. **One clear conversion.** Traffic should normally lead to the relevant website content and then to the early-access form.
5. **Measure confirmed consent.** A submitted form is not the final conversion; the primary conversion is a completed double opt-in.
6. **Stay product-neutral.** Until the final product is selected and substantiated, avoid claims about specifications, results, pain, permanence, cooling, clinical performance, price or launch date.

## 3. Audience hypotheses

These are starting hypotheses to test rather than fixed customer segments.

### Active home-IPL researchers

People already comparing at-home hair-removal options who want clear explanations, realistic expectations and help understanding treatment consistency.

### Premium beauty-technology buyers

People attracted to considered design, credible technology and a polished ownership experience, without relying on unsubstantiated luxury or performance claims.

### Consistency seekers

People who may have tried IPL before but struggled to understand the treatment cycle or maintain a routine.

Marketing should not infer or target sensitive medical information. Content must distinguish cosmetic education from medical advice.

## 4. The conversion journey

**Relevant reach → useful content → website visit → early-access form → confirmation email → confirmed subscriber**

Each campaign should have one intended next action. Social posts can educate natively, but their principal website link should point to the most relevant page rather than automatically sending everyone to the homepage.

## 5. Channel priorities

### Priority 1 — Search-led educational content

Create a small, high-quality library answering real questions about home IPL and the hair-growth cycle. This establishes a durable source of qualified traffic and gives social campaigns useful destinations.

Initial topics could include:

- the four stages of the hair-growth cycle;
- why consistency matters with home IPL;
- what anagen means in the context of IPL;
- IPL and laser hair removal: a careful, sourced explanation of the distinction;
- how to prepare for an at-home IPL routine;
- common home-IPL routine mistakes;
- general suitability considerations, using authoritative sources and avoiding personalised advice; and
- how to evaluate home-IPL claims responsibly.

Every article should have a clear author or reviewer, sources, an updated date, a concise early-access invitation and internal links to related content.

### Priority 2 — Organic Instagram and TikTok

Use short-form video and carousels to turn the brand's central idea into accessible, saveable education.

Recommended recurring formats:

- **Know the cycle:** one stage or concept explained simply;
- **IPL in 30 seconds:** a tightly scoped question and answer;
- **Myth / context:** correct a common misconception without making a product claim;
- **The routine:** consistency, planning and responsible preparation;
- **Building CYPH/1:** selective, transparent pre-launch progress; and
- **One useful fact:** a sourced insight that leads to a fuller website explanation.

Start with two or three short videos and one carousel per week. Sustainable quality is more important than daily publishing.

### Priority 3 — Pinterest

Test the strongest diagrams, cycle explainers and routine checklists as evergreen visual content. Use descriptive titles and link each pin to the corresponding website page.

### Priority 4 — Relevant creators and specialists

Begin with research and small collaborations rather than mass gifting. Prioritise creators whose audiences already discuss beauty technology, at-home routines or evidence-led skincare.

Before any endorsement or paid collaboration:

- provide a written claims and disclosure brief;
- require clear, upfront advertising disclosure where applicable;
- prohibit unsupported performance or medical claims;
- agree usage rights before reusing creator content; and
- direct traffic through a unique, consistently named UTM link.

### Priority 5 — Small paid-media experiments

Paid activity should begin only after organic content reveals a credible message and creative direction. Start with tightly capped experiments, not a broad launch campaign.

Suitable early tests include:

- search advertising against high-intent educational queries;
- broad or contextual social placements using proven organic creative; and
- amplification of a strong educational post.

Avoid behavioural retargeting or non-essential tracking until the consent, cookie and measurement approach has been reviewed and implemented appropriately.

## 6. Campaign territories

### Know the cycle

Explain why hair grows in stages and why a repeated routine matters. This is the clearest expression of the brand idea and can support search articles, video, carousels and email.

### Cycle. Phase. One.

Introduce the name and philosophy: understand the cycle, begin with the active-growth phase and approach treatment with consistency.

### Target the growth

Use as a high-level brand line, supported by careful explanatory copy. It must not imply that a device can detect the growth phase of an individual hair.

### The first 100

Use only if it refers to a genuine, defined early-access group. State clearly what membership means and do not imply a discount, product allocation or special benefit unless one has been approved and can be honoured.

## 7. First 90 days

### Weeks 1–2: measurement and foundations

- Verify Google Search Console and submit the XML sitemap.
- Record the starting position for organic impressions, visits and confirmed subscribers.
- Agree the UTM naming convention below.
- Define the first six social pieces and first two educational website articles.
- Create a simple claims register containing the source and approval status of every factual marketing claim.
- Confirm how acquisition source will be retained in Brevo without weakening the existing consent record.

### Weeks 3–6: organic message testing

- Publish the first educational pages.
- Run two or three recurring social formats.
- Test variations of the early-access invitation, changing one material variable at a time.
- Review results weekly, but judge search content over a longer period.
- Send no more than one useful subscriber update per month unless there is meaningful news.

### Weeks 7–10: partnership and paid pilots

- Approach a small shortlist of suitable creators for research or clearly disclosed collaboration.
- Put a limited test budget behind one or two organically proven concepts.
- Compare traffic quality and confirmed-signup cost, not reach alone.
- Stop placements that attract clicks but do not produce engaged visits or confirmed subscribers.

### Weeks 11–12: consolidate

- Identify the strongest audience, message, format and landing page combinations.
- Refresh or expand the best educational content.
- Document weak results and what they ruled out.
- Recommend whether to scale, refine or pause each channel for the next quarter.

## 8. Measurement framework

### Primary measures

- confirmed early-access subscribers;
- confirmed subscriber conversion rate;
- double-opt-in completion rate; and
- cost per confirmed subscriber when using paid media.

### Leading measures

- relevant website sessions by source;
- engaged visits to educational pages;
- early-access CTA clicks and form starts;
- social saves, shares and meaningful comments;
- video completion rate; and
- search impressions and click-through rate.

### Quality and trust measures

- unsubscribe rate;
- spam complaints;
- email delivery failures;
- misleading-claim or customer-service feedback; and
- accessibility or usability feedback affecting conversion.

Vanity reach and follower totals should be reported as context, not treated as the principal outcome.

## 9. UTM naming convention

Use lowercase values with hyphens and keep the taxonomy stable.

| Parameter | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `utm_source` | Platform or partner | `instagram`, `tiktok`, `google`, `creator-name` |
| `utm_medium` | Distribution type | `organic-social`, `paid-social`, `organic-search`, `creator` |
| `utm_campaign` | Strategic campaign | `prelaunch-know-the-cycle`, `prelaunch-first-100` |
| `utm_content` | Creative variation | `anagen-video-01`, `cycle-carousel-02` |

Do not put email addresses, names or other personal data in UTM parameters.

## 10. Initial experiment backlog

| Hypothesis | Test | Primary signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cycle education creates stronger intent than generic brand promotion. | Compare an anagen explainer with a brand-only introduction. | Confirmed subscribers per relevant visit |
| Saveable diagrams outperform talking-head explanations for this subject. | Publish the same concept as a carousel and short video. | Saves, completion and qualified clicks |
| A specific article is a better destination than the homepage. | Route matched creative to an educational page and compare with homepage traffic. | CTA rate and confirmed conversion |
| Routine language resonates more than hair-removal promises. | Compare responsible consistency messaging with a general category message. | Engagement quality and confirmed conversion |
| A proven organic concept can scale efficiently. | Run a small paid amplification test with a fixed cap. | Cost per confirmed subscriber |

## 11. Compliance and brand guardrails

- Keep all claims accurate, specific, evidenced and appropriate to a pre-launch brand.
- Label concept product imagery clearly and do not imply that it represents a final device.
- Do not use “expert”, “clinically proven”, “pain-free”, “permanent”, “best” or comparable claims without appropriate substantiation and approval.
- Clearly identify paid, gifted or otherwise controlled creator content as advertising.
- Collect marketing consent through the approved double-opt-in flow and make withdrawal straightforward.
- Use personal data only for the stated purpose and retain evidence of consent.
- Prefer aggregate, privacy-conscious measurement at this stage. Introduce non-essential analytics or advertising technologies only after a consent and privacy review.
- Review all suitability and safety content against authoritative guidance before publication.
