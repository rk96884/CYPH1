# CYPH/1 Graphic Language

**Status:** Approved  
**Date:** 20 August 2026

## Principle

The CYPH/1 graphic language should feel engineered, measured and quietly futuristic. Its distinctiveness comes from disciplined repetition of a few recognisable elements—not from decorative volume.

The core visual idea is **controlled cycles intersected by a precise target**. Circular phase geometry represents repetition and time; the angled follicle mark introduces direction, intervention and Phase One.

## Core motifs

### Cycle geometry

- Use complete circles, partial arcs and concentric rings to communicate cycles, phases and repeated treatment.
- Prefer one dominant circular construction rather than many decorative rings.
- Divide cycles into four only when the four hair-growth phases are being represented.
- Label scientific diagrams directly; never rely on colour alone.
- Broken arcs may indicate progression, but should not resemble loading indicators unless something is actually loading.

### Follicle gesture

- The approved follicle mark may be enlarged, cropped or used as a structural silhouette.
- Preserve its angle and proportions.
- Do not repeat the mark as a decorative pattern.
- Do not combine it with literal laser beams, sparkles or generic hair imagery.

### Phase notation

- Use restrained numerical notation such as `01 / ANAGEN` for ordered information.
- Use tabular alignment for phase numbers and technical sequences.
- Numbering supports hierarchy; it does not imply a product feature or performance claim.

## Line and shape system

- Structural hairline: 1px Alloy on dark or an equivalent accessible dark line on light.
- Emphasis line: 2px Signal lavender.
- Diagram stroke: 1–2px depending on rendered size.
- Prefer square geometry, open frames and ruled divisions.
- Use circular forms where they carry the cycle/follicle concept.
- Avoid generic rounded cards, floating capsules and decorative icon containers.
- Corners remain square or minimally softened only where touch controls or the physical product language requires it.

## Surfaces

- Obsidian and Carbon ink are the primary immersive canvases.
- Aubergine and Deep plum create low-contrast depth between dark sections.
- Soft white provides deliberate editorial contrast for science and long-form explanation.
- Section changes should use colour-field transitions, rules or whitespace—not stacks of cards.
- Use transparency sparingly; content must not depend on translucent glass effects.

## Metallic treatment

- Metallic detail is reserved for the approved logo, key campaign artwork and occasional fine rules.
- Build metallic effects from restrained tonal shifts between Soft white, Mist and Metallic lavender.
- Keep highlights narrow and directional.
- Functional UI, body text and scientific diagrams use flat colours.
- Avoid large chrome gradients, heavy bevels, bloom or persistent glow.

### Conditional champagne metal

- If the final device has an approved champagne or rose-gold finish, product-led compositions may use Champagne highlight, Rose alloy, Burnished gold and Deep bronze.
- Build the effect as a narrow directional transition from highlight through mid-tone to shadow; it should read as material, not a gold-coloured gradient fill.
- Signal Lavender remains the primary brand accent and continues to identify actions, focus and the follicle mark.
- Reserve champagne metal for hardware relationships, foil details, occasional fine rules and selected campaign moments.
- Do not use gold for body text, routine controls, scientific diagrams or broad section backgrounds.
- Until the device is confirmed, this treatment remains conditional and should not appear in the core website interface.

## Glow and light

- A subtle lavender ambient field may support a hero or product image.
- Glow never carries essential information or replaces contrast.
- Limit glow to one focal region within a composition.
- Avoid neon outlines around every component.

## Photography and product imagery

Photography should feel like premium consumer technology rather than a salon, clinic or cosmetics campaign.

Prefer:

- Controlled studio lighting and deep neutral backgrounds
- Material detail, engineering surfaces and precise crops
- Gender-neutral hands, environments and styling where people are shown
- Honest product scale and texture
- Negative space that supports concise typography

Avoid:

- Generic legs, hair strands or beauty poses
- Pink cosmetic lighting without purpose
- Medical-clinic cues
- Unrealistic product performance effects
- Imagery that implies an unconfirmed product, finish or capability

Until a final product is selected, use abstract brand imagery and educational diagrams rather than a fictional device render.

An anonymous product-direction teaser may be used before selection when it is clearly labelled `Product direction — final design subject to change`. It may show an abstract light-treatment aperture to make the intended product category legible, but must avoid identifiable manufacturer geometry and never define the aperture as IPL, laser or another specification unless the final product technology has been verified. It must never accompany specifications or performance claims.

## Iconography

- Use icons only when they improve comprehension or control recognition.
- Use a consistent 1.5–2px monoline construction.
- Prefer open geometry and simple forms.
- Pair unfamiliar icons with text.
- Do not place every icon inside a circle or rounded tile.
- Decorative icons, sparkles and pseudo-scientific symbols are not part of the system.

## Motion

- Standard interaction transitions: approximately 160–240ms.
- Larger editorial transitions: approximately 360–500ms.
- Use opacity, small translation and line progression with restrained easing.
- Circular motion may illustrate a real cycle or sequence; it should not loop decoratively.
- No scroll-jacking, parallax dependency or autoplay product video.
- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion` and preserve all information without animation.

## Composition

- Use deliberate asymmetry balanced by a strict grid.
- Keep one clear focal point per section.
- Allow generous negative space around headlines and the logo.
- Use rules and alignment to connect related information.
- Alternate immersive dark brand sections with light editorial science sections.
- Avoid dense card grids when a ruled list, sequence or open layout is clearer.

## Approval

Approved on 20 August 2026. The motifs, surface rules, imagery direction and motion principles are now the source for the project design tokens and subsequent website implementation. The champagne-metal extension remains conditional on confirmation of the final device finish.
